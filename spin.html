[01.CAMERA · CHARACTER · CONTROLS]

The 3Cs, as decisions

Bully · Rockstar · Controls

One button, one attacker at a time

Constraint
Combat used every face button. The challenge was to keep the depth and simplify it to one.
Call
A token system borrowed from kung fu movies. Many enemies surround you, but only the one holding the token attacks.
Result
Shipped in 2006. I was one of the four people who built the combat system, and I designed its bosses. Very similar systems were later seen in games like Arkham Asylum and Sleeping Dogs.

Resident Evil: Operation Raccoon City · Character and camera

Cover that never takes the controls away

Constraint
Snap-to-cover fell apart when swarmed. Players panicked and felt stuck to the wall.
Call
Cover is automatic and context sensitive. Controls and movement stay identical in or out of cover. Quick draw snaps your aim with a held button and the stick, without moving the camera.
Result
Shipped in 2012 on PS3 and Xbox 360, and sold 2.80 million units as of June 30, 2026.

Open-world prototype · Kennedy Miller Mitchell · Character

A combat system built on a real martial art

Constraint
A 1920s Shanghai open-world game in pre-production, with combat to design from nothing.
Call
Third-person combat based on Defendu, designed to grow as the player's skills unlock. The prototype added shooting and a chase through crowded alleys.
Result
Cancelled in pre-production. I also mentored the team of five designers.

Pistol Whip · Cloudhead · Controls in VR

Swing at bullets. Play the gun.

Constraint
A live rhythm-shooter that needed new ways to play without losing its raw simplicity.
Call
Deflector lets you swing at bullets and send them back. The Drum Gun plays a different drum sound for each shot, hit and reload, and your hand movement changes it.
Result
Drum Gun shipped in the November 2023 anniversary update. Deflector shipped in June 2024.

[05.SYSTEMIC · OPEN AND SEMI-OPEN]

Systems, and what I learned about scope

  • BullyOpen world

    Scripted NPC navigation and ambient NPC interactions, designed the social reputation system, and scripted mini-games, including Future Street Race 2165.

  • SOCOM: Fireteam Bravo 3Squad AI

    Designed the AI for teammates and enemies, directed the single-player level design and story, and brought squad commands over from Tactical Strike. Teammates had to stay out of the way of your shot and still look effective.

  • Journey to FoundationSemi-open levels

    Halfway through development we found our semi-open, multi-path levels were not feasible on the target hardware, and they were cut back to linear. The lesson I took: understand the target hardware's technical limits before building levels around them.
